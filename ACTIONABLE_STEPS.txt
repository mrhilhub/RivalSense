# RivalSense SaaS Launch Roadmap

## Vision

Build **RivalSense** into the definitive AI industry intelligence platform—a searchable, continuously updated database that tracks AI companies, products, pricing, model releases, funding, hiring, acquisitions, documentation changes, and competitive intelligence in one place.

The goal is to become the first place people go to answer questions about the AI ecosystem without having to search dozens of websites.

# Phase 3 – Security & Stability

Before accepting paying customers:

* Authenticate every API endpoint.
* Never trust user IDs sent from the client.
* Enforce Supabase Row Level Security on every table.
* Protect cron endpoints with a secret.
* Validate URLs to prevent SSRF attacks.
* Add rate limiting.
* Improve error handling and logging.
* Monitor API usage and performance.
* Create database backups.

Reliability builds customer confidence.

It's **possible**, but I would not make **Oracle** the goal.

I'd make the goal:

> **Build a company that multiple enterprise software companies would want to acquire.**

A $10 million acquisition is far more about traction and strategic value than the idea itself.

### What Oracle would care about

Oracle doesn't typically buy products because they're "cool." They buy companies that help them:

* Sell more cloud services.
* Add valuable enterprise data.
* Strengthen AI or analytics offerings.
* Acquire customers or talented engineering teams.

For RivalSense, the interesting angle isn't "AI news." It's becoming **the structured intelligence layer for the AI market**.

### What gets acquired for $10M+

Generally, you'd want something like:

* $500k–$2M in annual recurring revenue (ARR), depending on growth and strategic fit.
* Thousands of engaged users, especially enterprise users.
* High-quality proprietary data that's difficult to replicate.
* Strong customer retention.
* A recognizable brand in your niche.

A company with $1M ARR that's growing 100% year over year could plausibly receive acquisition interest in the high seven or low eight figures, though there are no guarantees.

### I actually see a stronger opportunity

Right now, RivalSense is described as "tracking AI companies."

That's useful.

But imagine if it became:

> **The operating system for AI market intelligence.**

Not just tracking websites, but answering questions like:

* "Which AI startups added MCP support this month?"
* "Which companies changed pricing in the last 90 days?"
* "Which open-source AI companies are growing fastest?"
* "Who competes with Cursor, and how do their features compare?"
* "Show me every funding round in AI agents this quarter."

Now you're building something much harder to copy.

### Potential acquirers

I actually think these categories are more likely than Oracle if RivalSense succeeds:

* Enterprise software and CRM companies.
* Business intelligence and analytics vendors.
* Market intelligence platforms.
* AI infrastructure companies.
* Cloud providers.
* Developer tooling companies.

Oracle is one possibility, but there are many others that could find a platform like this strategically valuable.

### A realistic five-year roadmap

**Year 1**

* Polish the product.
* Reach 100 paying customers.
* Build proprietary data and AI search.

**Year 2**

* Reach $250k–$500k ARR.
* Launch APIs and enterprise features.
* Become known in the AI ecosystem.

**Years 3–4**

* Expand to thousands of customers.
* Build strong retention and recurring revenue.
* Develop data assets that competitors can't easily reproduce.

**Years 4–5**

* If the business has meaningful revenue, proprietary data, and strategic customers, acquisition conversations become much more realistic.

### One thing I'd change

Don't optimize for getting acquired.

Optimize for building a company that **doesn't need to be acquired**.

Ironically, those are often the companies that receive the best acquisition offers. If RivalSense becomes indispensable to founders, investors, analysts, and enterprise teams, you'll have more options—whether that's continuing to grow independently or entertaining acquisition interest when it makes strategic sense.
